02 / DEVICE ROUTE

How was it reset?

Examine the damaged Fold2 and relevant Samsung/Google account activity for available reset, remote-wipe, activation or first-setup traces. Their presence and retention are not guaranteed.

EXAMINATION NEEDED

Photograph the damage first and preserve the device with chain of custody. A qualified examiner checks whether a reset occurred, approximate wipe/first-boot time, and whether a local or remote wipe is indicated. Obtain account sign-in and remote-action history with lawful consent or process. A broken or encrypted phone may reveal little.

RECORD NEEDED

Request original camera recordings, time-offset checks, access-control logs and guard testimony. Map what each camera actually covers. These can corroborate entry/exit, not who handled the phone inside a private room unless that is captured.

04 / SECURITY CONTROL

FRP ≠ proof of actor

A Settings reset typically asks for a lock credential. A recovery-button reset is also possible; Google's Factory Reset Protection can then restrict reuse. Neither alone identifies who pressed reset.

TECHNICAL CONTEXT

Compare the lock state, reset method and FRP/setup state, plus account logs. Settings wipe typically requires authentication; recovery wipe and remote erase are alternatives. FRP may block subsequent setup but does not identify the person who initiated the wipe.

Important distinction. The four-minute gap between the 12:18 mobile call and 12:22 landline report is compatible with the scenario's sequence, but it does not by itself prove who wiped or broke the phone, exactly when it happened, or anyone's motive. Preserve the handset and request records lawfully; do not infer guilt from a call log alone.
