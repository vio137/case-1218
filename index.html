01 — The short answer

The strongest hypothesis

From the story alone it cannot be conclusively determined. But one explanation towers over the others. HYPOTHESIS — MUST BE PROVED

The wife had custody of her Galaxy Z Fold 2 at 12:18 AM, made or was associated with the 10-second call, the phone was reset shortly after, then broken — and she called police from the landline at 12:22 AM. 3No reset command came from his devices or accounts.
4She had opportunity, custody, motive — and inconsistent conduct.

06 — The decisive technical question

Local or remote?

On a modern encrypted Samsung, a factory reset destroys the local encryption keys — so the handset may never give the data back. The investigation lives in cloud logs, telecom records, account activity and device metadata. First question for the forensic lab: which of the four reset paths was used? FSL DETERMINES THIS

Factory reset through Settings

Settings → General Management → Reset → Factory Data Reset

What it requires

  • Phone powered on, screen functional
  • Unlocked — PIN / biometric authentication
  • Multiple confirmations
  • Physical access to the device

Tactical importance

  • Whoever did it had both the phone and the unlock method
  • If her PIN was known only to her and the husband never had the phone — this path points at her

Factory reset through Recovery Mode

Hardware key combination — on some Samsung models a USB/PC connection is required

What it requires

  • Physical possession
  • Technical knowledge, button sequence
  • Possibly a PC / USB cable
  • Works even on a locked phone — but triggers FRP afterwards

Tactical importance

  • Check for a laptop/PC, USB cable, connection logs on any computer
  • Search histories for "hard reset Samsung"
  • No PC, cable or trace linking the husband helps him

Remote erase — Google Find My Device

Requires: linked Google account · account access · device online

What Google can disclose

  • Erase command and its exact time
  • Source device, source IP, browser identifier
  • Account login history and security alerts
  • Device registration / check-in events

Tactical importance

  • Command from her own session → points at her
  • Command from the other man's device/IP → points at him with her credentials
  • Nothing from the husband's devices/IPs → his innocence evidence

Remote erase — Samsung Find My Mobile

Requires: linked Samsung account · remote features enabled · account credentials

What Samsung can disclose

  • Remote erase / unlock / locate commands + timestamps
  • Source IP and device/browser used
  • Account login history, security alerts
  • Samsung Cloud backup & sync activity

Tactical importance

  • Did she back up before the reset? Preserving what she "destroyed" is consciousness of guilt
  • Was a new device registered to her account after the reset?
  • No command from the husband's side → helps him
This is decisive: if the reset was remote, account logs identify the source. If it was local, custody and unlock credentials decide it. The defence formally asks the lab to distinguish Settings reset / Recovery reset / Google erase / Samsung erase / MDM / any other method.

08 — The attack, lawfully

Seven inconsistencies

The defence attacks inconsistencies, not character. Each one is a question the prosecution must answer. Tap to open. INFERENCE — REASONABLE DOUBT

01

The surprise return

He flew back early from Dubai to surprise her for her birthday — which explains why she did not expect him, and why she was caught.
Evidence: flight itinerary, boarding pass, immigration entry, passport stamp, taxi receipt, society entry, gift receipt, messages about the surprise plan.
02

The club, 4 PM to 11:45 PM

He waited in the society club nearly eight hours — waiting for midnight. Which also means: no opportunity to enter the flat before 11:55 PM.
Evidence: club CCTV, staff statements, entry/exit register, payment records, Wi-Fi logs, his phone's location history.
03

The other man walks out at 12:10

Alive, mobile, unharmed, in haste, carrying his clothes. That supports "found them, told him to leave" — and nothing more violent.
Evidence: CCTV, guard statement, gate record. Whether he carried a phone matters for the remote-wipe hypothesis.
04

The 12:18 call — to him, not the police

Minutes after an alleged attempted rape, her first call is not 100. It is ten seconds to the man who just left. Why?
The prosecution must explain: why call the lover first, what fit in ten seconds, whether the call was mobile or landline, and why both phones were wiped immediately after. A major point for the husband.
05

Two phones, wiped in sync

Her phone and his phone reset around the same time, right after a ten-second call. That is coordination.
Questions: who reset each phone, local or remote, was the wipe command sent from the other man's device, did they share credentials, was the purpose to erase call logs and messages?
06

The phone is broken after the reset

A wiped phone is already empty. Breaking it afterwards destroys the physical evidence of when and how the reset happened — and adds "his violence" to the story.
Questions: who broke it, where were the pieces, who handed it to police, does she have glass cuts, does he? Was it broken before or after the reset?
07

The landline call at 12:22

The control room recording is her first version — before lawyers, before the FIR. Calm or distressed? Consistent or not?
Examine: was the husband audible, did she say he was present, did she mention the phone, does it match the FIR, how long until police arrived, was the scene recorded immediately?

    Eight pillars

    TIMELINEEarly return for a birthday surprise, the club until 11:45 PM, entry at 11:55 PM, the discovery, the man told to leave.
    CUSTODYProve he never had custody or control of her phone at the time of the reset and breaking.

    THE OTHER MANDid the 10-second call trigger coordinated resets or a remote wipe? His phone and records answer it.
    NO FORENSIC SUPPORTMedical, DNA, injury evidence — its absence weakens the attempted-rape allegation.
    PRESERVATIONCCTV, telecom, cloud, router, smart lock, medical, devices — preserved before they disappear.
    The wife had custody and opportunity, made a short call to the other man, and shortly afterwards her phone was reset and broken — before she called the police from the landline. This creates a strong inference that she reset and broke the phone to destroy evidence of the relationship, and then alleged attempted rape to explain the situation.
    • No remote erase from the husband's accounts or devices
    • No local reset trace linking the husband
    • Wife had custody of the phone and knew the PIN
    • The 12:18 call came from her working phone
    • The wife broke the phone — or cannot show he did
    • No medical evidence of attempted rape
    • The other man's records show a coordinated reset

    If the evidence shows these, the husband has strong reasonable doubt — and a record of false implication. The investigation must answer: who had the phone, how was it reset, when, from which account or device, and who broke it.
